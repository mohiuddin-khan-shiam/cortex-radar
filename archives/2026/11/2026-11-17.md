<summary><b>Abstract & Metadata</b></summary>

### [Latent Lattice Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21608)
- **Category:** 🧠 LLMs & Reasoning (`llm_reasoning`)
- **Authors:** Shivam Duggal, Zongze Wu, William T. Freeman, Antonio Torralba et al. (+2)
- **Signal Score:** `9.6/10.0`
- **Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21608) | [Direct PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.21608v1)

**TL;DR:** We introduce the Lattice of Latents, a set of per-layer horizontal latent streams that run recurrently along time, alongside the vertical residual stream that flows through depth.

<details>
<summary><b>Abstract & Metadata</b></summary>

Language models typically increase per-token computation through deeper or looped layers and latent reasoning steps, while richer residual connections improve information flow across layers. We instead increase computational capacity: the number of active computational states a model maintains. We introduce the Lattice of Latents, a set of per-layer horizontal latent streams that run recurrently along time, alongside the vertical residual stream that flows through depth. Like hyper-connections, mHC, and attention or value residuals, the Lattice enhances the residual stream, but rather than adding or rerouting residual pathways through depth, it provides companion computational states that persist across tokens. Unlike hybrid Transformer-SSM architectures, whose recurrent states primarily serve as internal memory for sequence mixing, these latents directly condition the vertical stream's attention, KV-cache writes, MLP computation, and decoding. On Transformers trained on 20B FineWeb-Edu tokens, a 406M-parameter Lattice model lowers validation perplexity from 15.41 to 14.40 over a parameter-matched Transformer. These gains extend across architectural variants. The Lattice composes with prior residual-stream enhancements: with hyper-connections and input residuals, it lowers validation perplexity from 15.41 (base Transformer) and 14.22 (Transformer with the same enhancements) to 13.61. On Gated DeltaNet, Mamba-2, Triadic GDN, and GDN-Mamba-2 hybrids, it improves perplexity at every tested native state size, with Lattice-GDN and Lattice-Triadic-GDN variants outperforming pure baselines with 4x larger native states. These results identify persistent per-layer state as a complementary axis for scaling language-model computation.

### [ViRA: Toward Ultra-High-Resolution Visual Agents via Cross-Input Relative Policy Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21605)
- **Category:** 🎯 RL & Optimization (`reinforcement_learning`)
- **Authors:** Huawei Lin, Jinting Liu, Tongyan Wang, Xuyang Chen et al. (+8)
- **Signal Score:** `9.6/10.0`
- **Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21605) | [Direct PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.21605v1)

**TL;DR:** We propose Cross-Input Relative Policy Optimization (CrossRPO), which trains agents on contrast sets.

<details>
<summary><b>Abstract & Metadata</b></summary>

Visual agents answer questions about ultra-high-resolution photos by zooming into the relevant regions. However, existing agents often answer from common sense instead of reading the evidence. Reinforcement learning (RL) on the final answer cannot correct this, because a guess earns the same reward as reading. We propose Cross-Input Relative Policy Optimization (CrossRPO), which trains agents on contrast sets. A contrast set asks one question about several versions of an image that differ only in a small region with the evidence and have different answers, so only reading the evidence solves the whole set. CrossRPO rewards solving the whole set, and we prove that it does not reinforce a set answered from common sense. We build contrast sets for training (ViRA-Train) and evaluation (ViRA-Bench) by editing one small object in images of 21 to 120 megapixels, and we photograph further sets with real changes (ViRA-Bench-Real) to exclude editing traces. We train two Visual Reasoning Agents, ViRA-9B and ViRA-27B, with CrossRPO on only about 3,500 items of ViRA-Train each. CrossRPO achieves 63.3% set accuracy on ViRA-Bench at the maximum resolution, against 59.3% for GRPO. ViRA-9B is the most accurate open model of its size on all benchmarks, and ViRA-27B outperforms Claude Haiku 5.5 and GPT-6-Luna, both at their highest thinking level, on most benchmarks.

<details>
<summary><b>Abstract & Metadata</b></summary>

Training with MXFP4, a 4-bit microscaling format, is non-deterministic: identical inputs can produce different results across parallelism layouts and GPU vendors. We present CertiSum to make MXFP4 training bitwise invariant across runs, parallelism layouts, and GPU vendors. <summary><b>Abstract & Metadata</b></summary>

Safe in-context reinforcement learning (Safe ICRL) asks a policy with fixed parameters to improve on an unseen constrained task from its interaction history while aiming to keep its cost within a budget. <summary><b>Abstract & Metadata</b></summary>

Long video generation should keep the subject and background consistent while supporting rich, natural motion. Reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards inherits whatever its verifier gets wrong.
