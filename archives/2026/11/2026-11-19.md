### [Phoenix: Learning Scene Structure Representations for Generalizable World-Action Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24938)
- **Category:** 👁️ Vision & Multimodal (`vision_multimodal`)
- **Authors:** Yupeng Zheng, Chaoyue Li, Linbo Wang, Xiang Li et al. (+14)
- **Signal Score:** `9.6/10.0`
- **Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24938) | [Direct PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24938v1)

**TL;DR:** We introduce Phoenix, which learns representations of scene structure for world-action modeling.

<details>
<summary><b>Abstract & Metadata</b></summary>

World-action models jointly predict future observations and actions, yet reconstruction-oriented visual representations do not explicitly emphasize the spatial relationships and object-state changes needed for manipulation. We introduce Phoenix, which learns representations of scene structure for world-action modeling. Specifically, Phoenix Encoder enriches DINOv3's general visual semantics through dual-teacher distillation of geometric and spatiotemporal semantic knowledge. Next, CS-VAE causally aggregates and compresses Phoenix Encoder features into generative latents while preserving scene information and spatial layout. Finally, Phoenix jointly predicts these latents, complementary appearance latents, and robot actions, linking scene evolution with action generation. To study pretraining data efficiency, we curate approximately 39,000 hours of egocentric and robot manipulation data for representation learning and use the robot demonstration subset for WAM pretraining. Following benchmark-specific post-training, Phoenix achieves state-of-the-art performance across simulation and real-robot benchmarks spanning single-arm, bimanual, and dexterous manipulation. It demonstrates strong robustness to perturbations, achieving success rates of 94.8% on LIBERO-Plus and 72.4% on RoboTwin 2.0 C2R, alongside strong cross-embodiment transfer and real-world manipulation performance on Bench2Dex and RoboDojo-RealWorld, respectively. Pretraining-scale analysis shows larger performance gains from data scaling than a video-VAE-only baseline, indicating stronger scaling benefits and more effective data utilization. Moreover, Phoenix maintains high success rates when adapted with limited downstream demonstrations, demonstrating strong few-shot adaptation. Our code, pretraining data, and models are available on the project page to help the community reproduce our results.

### [$Z$-sight: Unifying Vision-Language-Action and Latent World Modeling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24940)
- **Category:** 👁️ Vision & Multimodal (`vision_multimodal`)
- **Authors:** Junjie Ye, Yiqi Zhao, Muchen Xu, Celina Shiyu Wang et al. (+11)
- **Signal Score:** `9.2/10.0`
- **Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24940) | [Direct PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24940v1)

**TL;DR:** We introduce $Z$-sight, a unified robot foundation model that couples vision-language-action (VLA) and generative latent world modeling.

<details>
<summary><b>Abstract & Metadata</b></summary>

We introduce $Z$-sight, a unified robot foundation model that couples vision-language-action (VLA) and generative latent world modeling. Video-based world action models (WAMs) show the value of joint future-and-action generation for control, yet inherit the heavy cost of video generators. Recent latent-space policies avoid pixel generation, yet typically align intermediate features or rely on frozen target encoders, decoupling future prediction from the VLM's native feature space. $Z$-sight instead predicts the future directly in the visual token space in which its VLM perceives the present. Coupling a foresight expert and a continuous action expert with the VLM in a mixture-of-transformers, $Z$-sight jointly denoises future spatial tokens and actions at inference without pixel reconstruction or external encoders. Trained on human video and robot trajectories, $Z$-sight achieves 81.9% success on SIMPLE (+9.6 points over the strongest baseline) and 73.3% across six Unitree G1 tasks (+20.0 points) at 110 ms per chunk, while remaining competitive on tabletop benchmarks.

### [Agentic Harness Engineering Beta: Requirement-Centered Evolution of Agent Harnesses](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24924)
- **Category:** 🤖 Agents & Systems (`agentic_systems`)
- **Authors:** Jiahang Lin, Jiahao Chen, Yiding Wang, Shichun Liu et al. (+10)
- **Signal Score:** `8.9/10.0`
- **Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24924) | [Direct PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24924v1)

**TL;DR:** We introduce Agentic Harness Engineering Beta (AHE-Beta), a requirement-centered loop that brings real-world engineering practices into harness evolution.

<details>
<summary><b>Abstract & Metadata</b></summary>

Real-world harness engineering is driven by explicit and implicit user requirements, which guide development and validation. However, existing harness evolution methods often overlook implicit requirements and interaction needs, while using a single evolution agent to modify the harness and benchmark scores to select changes. This process often produces narrow patches and leads to benchmark-specific local optima. We introduce Agentic Harness Engineering Beta (AHE-Beta), a requirement-centered loop that brings real-world engineering practices into harness evolution. The loop consists of three steps: (1) User-driven evaluation lets simulated users raise their requirements as issues while they work with the harness over multiple turns. (2) Team-based modification implements those requirements as generalizable changes rather than task-specific patches. (3) Requirement-driven selection merges a change only when it resolves a verified requirement and passes review. Empirically, on held-out tasks, AHE-Beta raises pass@1 from 21.9% to 33.3% while using 40.8% fewer tokens than Base Harness, and achieves higher pass@1 than Claude Code, Meta-Harness, and AHE. In a blind human A/B test, real users prefer AHE-Beta in 31 of 48 valid pairs and rate it higher on all five dimensions, matching the directional preference of the user simulator. The human A/B results show that AHE-Beta achieves its goal of aligning harness evolution with real-world user requirements. Without further evolution, the gains transfer to GLM-5.3 and DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash with pass@1 improvements of 15.8% and 5.3% over Base Harness. Together, these results show that requirement-centered harness evolution is a more general method for improving agent capabilities.

### [PrismRestore: A Compact Restoration Toolbox for Agentic Image Restoration](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24915)
- **Category:** 🎯 RL & Optimization (`reinforcement_learning`)
- **Authors:** Zhaocheng Yu, Kui Jiang, Jing Cao, Yiang Chen et al. (+3)
- **Signal Score:** `8.9/10.0`
- **Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24915) | [Direct PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24915v1)

**TL;DR:** We propose PrismRestore, which constructs a compact bank of callable restoration actions from a single frozen all-in-one model.

<details>
<summary><b>Abstract & Metadata</b></summary>

Agentic image restoration composes operations to handle mixed degradations, yet its toolbox often requires storing multiple restoration networks. We propose PrismRestore, which constructs a compact bank of callable restoration actions from a single frozen all-in-one model. Our approach builds on task-dependent yet overlapping channel responses in the shared model. An offline task-prior search uses activation and gradient statistics to group channels with similar response profiles, then searches task-specific modulation coefficients over non-representative channels while leaving representative channels unmodulated. The resulting prior bank enables explicit action selection and composition without additional restoration backbones or task-specific adapter modules. A state-conditioned policy learns to compose these actions using Group Relative Policy Optimization. Its objective separately normalizes fidelity and perceptual feedback within groups of trajectories sampled for the same input, and combines terminal evaluation with return-to-go derived from stepwise quality changes. Experiments on synthetic composite-degradation and real-world multi-task benchmarks show improvements over the evaluated all-in-one baselines and restoration quality competitive with multi-model agents. PrismRestore achieves these results with a single restoration backbone and faster inference than the evaluated agentic baselines.

### [On Evaluating User Simulators for Interactive Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24892)

- **Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24892) | [Direct PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24892v1)

**TL;DR:** User simulators offer a scalable way to evaluate interactive agents, but how to assess their quality remains underexplored.

<details>
<summary><b>Abstract & Metadata</b></summary>

User simulators offer a scalable way to evaluate interactive agents, but how to assess their quality remains underexplored. In particular, it is unclear whether existing user simulator evaluation frameworks reliably detect meaningful failures in simulated user behavior. This uncertainty makes it difficult to interpret simulator evaluation. To understand what a high benchmark score tells us about simulator quality, we (meta-)evaluate the metrics themselves. To instantiate this meta-evaluation, we deliberately perturb simulated replays to induce failures such as altered goals, omitted constraints, premature stopping, and acceptance of broken work, then test whether the metrics penalize these failures. Evaluation methods from three prior studies detect only 5-6 of the 10 perturbations; their combined scores even favor populations instructed to accept broken work over unmodified controls. Motivated by these gaps, we propose a new evaluation suite that combines utility of user simulators for evaluating agents and two measures of fidelity to real users: population-level fidelity assesses collective interaction patterns, while trace-level fidelity assesses the preservation of individual users' goals, constraints, and persona. Under the same detection criterion, our evaluation suite detects all 10 perturbations. Evaluating six user simulators on 351 real multi-turn sessions in which 348 distinct users across 23 languages and agents work together to create and refine artifacts such as full-stack web apps, games, design assets, and automated workflows, we find that population-level measures can miss when a simulated user pursues the wrong goal, ignores a constraint, or behaves out of character, highlighting the need to assess fidelity at comprehensive granularities.

<summary><b>Abstract & Metadata</b></summary>

Most existing approaches enhance mathematical reasoning in large language models (LLMs) as a monolithic capability, despite the fact that different mathematical problems require different reasoning skills. Neural surrogates for external vehicle aerodynamics are fast alternatives to physics-based computational fluid dynamics (CFD) simulations. Similar to pretrained models in other fields, a pretrained surrogate should adapt to a new vehicle class using only a few simulations and stay accurate on its pretraining domain.
