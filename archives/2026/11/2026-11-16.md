### [Training Reasoning Models in Simulation to Natively Use Real Robots](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18562)
- **Category:** 🧠 LLMs & Reasoning (`llm_reasoning`)
- **Authors:** Jiaming Tang, Zhuoyang Zhang, Junxian Guo, Fangzhou Zhao et al. (+7)
- **Signal Score:** `9.8/10.0`
- **Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18562) | [Direct PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.18562v1)

**TL;DR:** We show that an open-weight 27B reasoning model can be trained to use real robots natively through reinforcement learning, entirely in simulation and without demonstrations.

<details>
<summary><b>Abstract & Metadata</b></summary>

Robot-use agents (RUAs) have emerged as a promising approach to general-purpose robot control. However, strong performance has so far been achieved only by frontier proprietary models such as GPT-6 Astra and Claude Fable 5.2, whereas open-weight models remain substantially less capable. We show that an open-weight 27B reasoning model can be trained to use real robots natively through reinforcement learning, entirely in simulation and without demonstrations. Like a computer-use agent (CUA), the model observes the environment, reasons in a chain of thought, and generates the next end-effector pose, without any harness. We build a Post-training Engine for Agentic Robotics (PEAR) and use it to train the model on simulated robot tasks at scale with robot-use reinforcement learning (RURL), mirroring how frontier models acquire computer use. We find that the skills acquired in simulation generalize well beyond the training tasks: they transfer to an unseen robot, to the real world, and to novel instructions. After training on LIBERO and RoboDojo in simulation, the model succeeds in 60% of our trials on a real dual-arm robot, whereas the base model succeeds in none. On LIBERO, training raises the success rate from 1.6% to 74.4%, approaching GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol at maximum reasoning effort. Compared with $π_{0.5}$, a vision-language-action model fine-tuned on demonstrations, our model is substantially more robust to changes in the task itself. On LIBERO-Pro, $π_{0.5}$ succeeds in only 0.75% of episodes when assigned a new task in a familiar scene whereas our model succeeds in 57.4%. Finally, the model retains its general capabilities, with no measurable degradation on nine reasoning, coding and vision benchmarks. These results highlight robot-use reinforcement learning as a promising component of foundation-model post-training.

<details>
<summary><b>Abstract & Metadata</b></summary>

Small language models are attractive for local, low-cost, and privacy-sensitive deployment, but they remain unreliable on mathematical reasoning tasks that require decomposition, exact computation, tool use, and verification. We introduce \textbf{ARCHER}: \textbf{A}ction-trace \textbf{R}einforcement for \textbf{C}alculator-grounded \textbf{H}ierarchical \textbf{E}xecutable \textbf{R}easoning, a two-stage framework for training small language models (SLMs) to produce executable and auditable reasoning trajectories. In the first stage, ARCHER compiles free-form chain-of-thought rationales into typed action traces containing reasoning steps, calculator calls, deterministic runtime observations, verification actions, and final answers. This shifts the supervision target from imitating explanatory text to learning a tool-grounded reasoning policy. Evaluations on two additional reasoning-specialized 7B backbones further show that executable grounding remains beneficial after reasoning-focused reinforcement learning or distillation. These results demonstrate that executable supervision and reward-guided behavioral refinement provide a general and effective training paradigm for compact mathematical reasoners.

### [MMFlash: Faster Speculative Decoding for Vision-Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18482)

**TL;DR:** we propose MMFlash, a simple and effective framework that adaptively upweights visually dependent tokens that the drafter predicts poorly.

<details>
<summary><b>Abstract & Metadata</b></summary>

Speculative decoding accelerates generation by letting a lightweight drafter propose tokens for parallel verification by a target model. Diffusion drafters such as DFlash propose an entire token block in a single forward pass, but mainly focus on text-only tasks, leaving multimodal drafting underexplored. To study this setting, we first introduce a metric of token-level visual dependence based on the KL divergence between next-token distributions with and without visual input. We find that acceptance rates decrease from 46% in the lowest visual-dependence quartile to 22% in the highest, with this disparity persisting after multimodal training. Motivated by this observation, we propose MMFlash, a simple and effective framework that adaptively upweights visually dependent tokens that the drafter predicts poorly. For long video inputs, MMFlash further employs sparse attention to reduce redundant attention computation and improve drafting quality. Across various multimodal benchmarks, MMFlash achieves lossless decoding speedups of up to $3.58\times$, outperforming EAGLE-3 and DFlash by up to 195.4% and 17.1%, respectively.

### [Restoring Diversity in Distilled Image and Video Generators by Probing Activations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18550)
- **Category:** 👁️ Vision & Multimodal (`vision_multimodal`)
- **Authors:** Furkan Horoz, Ozgur Kara, Kaan Oktay, Adil Kaan Akan et al. (+3)
- **Signal Score:** `9.2/10.0`
- **Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18550) | [Direct PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.18550v1)

**TL;DR:** we propose ReDiverse to reinstate the missing variance directions.

<details>
<summary><b>Abstract & Metadata</b></summary>

Distilling diffusion models into few-step student models substantially reduces sampling latency, but induces severe diversity collapse: samples generated from different random seeds show minimal variation. In this work, we investigate the root cause of this failure by probing the internal representations of student models relative to their teachers. We find that distilled students maintain total activation variance comparable to their teachers, but concentrate it into substantially fewer directions. This directional collapse is localized to an identifiable intermediate block at the initial denoising step. Consequently, diversity collapse stems from a misallocation of activation variance rather than an inherent loss of generative capacity. Guided by this finding, we propose ReDiverse to reinstate the missing variance directions. At training time, ReDiverse-FT fine-tunes a lightweight adapter that regresses student activations onto the teacher's, reviving the teacher's feature variance characteristic. At inference time, ReDiverse-TF introduces a training-free mechanism that steers activations along precomputed teacher principal directions. Across image and video distillation benchmarks, ReDiverse consistently restores sample diversity while preserving generation quality, further supported by the user study.

### [StreamEmbed: Real-Time Indexing from Pixels for Screenshot Retrieval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18516)

- **Signal Score:** `9.2/10.0`
- **Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18516) | [Direct PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.18516v1)

**TL;DR:** We present StreamEmbed, a system for incremental visual embedding that exploits this redundancy.

<details>
<summary><b>Abstract & Metadata</b></summary>

Screen history can serve as searchable visual memory for people and agents, but unlike an offline corpus, it must be encoded and indexed as each screenshot arrives. OCR-based indexing is fast but loses visual information; visual encoding takes over two seconds. Yet screens change incrementally: most pixels repeat within and across screenshots. This raises a question: do visual encoders need to recompute their representations from scratch for every new screenshot? We present StreamEmbed, a system for incremental visual embedding that exploits this redundancy. Guided solely by pixel differences, it combines spatial token pruning, which skips blocks that repeat a neighbor, with temporal KV reuse, which reuses cached keys and values for blocks unchanged across screenshots. A small learned adapter compensates for pruning and reuse to preserve fidelity to full-screenshot embeddings. On ScreenMemEval, our benchmark of 10,426 questions over 184 hours of screen histories from two humans and a computer-use agent, StreamEmbed sets a new accuracy-latency Pareto frontier on an M2 Pro GPU: it encodes up to 3.7x faster than full-screenshot encoding without sacrificing accuracy and improves accuracy over OCR-based indexing by up to 6.3 Recall@10 points at matched latency. A memory-efficient vector index stores a simulated year of screenshots in 0.13 GB of RAM. Together, these results make real-time indexing of screen history in its native visual form practical, laying a foundation for fully local visual assistants.

<details>
<summary><b>Abstract & Metadata</b></summary>

Agentic reinforcement learning (RL) increasingly relies on a harness, the scaffold around the model that decides what it sees of its own interaction and what guidance it receives. Most existing approaches train under a hand-written harness that stays fixed throughout. However, an effective harness depends on the domain, the data and the model, and keeps changing as the model trains. We introduce ModHarness-RL, which recasts the harness as programs that are learned from data and evolve as the model trains with RL. ModHarness-RL records the interaction as structured State, a set of typed blocks, and two core components of the harness are programs over these blocks, a Compression program that assembles the accumulated history into the model's context and a Steering program that supplies guidance when behavior deviates. During RL training, candidate programs are proposed from recent trajectories, verified by execution, and replayed over past trajectories, so the harness tracks how the model's behavior shifts. We study CodeSearch in depth and further analyze ALFWorld and BIRD SQL, with models from 1.5B to 8B. The proposed Compression and Steering mechanisms improve held-out performance over the fixed-harness baseline in most settings. These results suggest that a harness for agentic RL can be learned from the task's data and evolved with the model.

**TL;DR:** Demonstrations are an effective way to accelerate single-agent reinforcement learning (RL), but extending this idea to multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL)...

<details>
<summary><b>Abstract & Metadata</b></summary>

Demonstrations are an effective way to accelerate single-agent reinforcement learning (RL), but extending this idea to multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) is non-trivial as it is difficult and often impossible for a single person to teleoperate multiple agents simultaneously. Round-Robin Behavior Cloning (R2BC) addresses this by letting a single demonstrator control one agent at a time while the others execute their current policies. However, the resulting imitation policies are limited by the quality of the demonstrations. We propose Imitation Bootstrapped Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (IBMARL), a framework that extends Imitation Bootstrapped Reinforcement Learning (IBRL) to cooperative teams and uses R2BC to train the imitation policies. At each time step, every agent receives one action proposal from its imitation policy and one from its RL policy, and an ensemble of centralized critics selects among the per-agent combinations of the two. A gated imitation penalty, a soft Q-filtered behavior cloning loss, regularizes each RL policy toward its imitation policy only where the critics value the imitation action more, so that after training each agent executes its RL policy alone, without the centralized critics. On four cooperative multi-agent RL benchmarks, IBMARL surpasses its imitation policies $2.6$-$4.2\times$ faster than demonstration-based baselines on two tasks, is the only method to surpass it on the other two, and exceeds the demonstrator itself on two of the four. With real human demonstrations IBMARL still surpasses imitation learning alone, and does so more than twice as fast as the best baseline.

<summary><b>Abstract & Metadata</b></summary>

Generative modeling based on stochastic interpolants requires specifying two key choices: an interpolant schedule bridging the initial and target distributions, and an efficient discretization of the resulting probability-flow ODE. ### [Alignment by Socialization: Learning Tradeoffs Under Selection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18531)

<details>
<summary><b>Abstract & Metadata</b></summary>

Artificial intelligence (AI) agents are expected to complete tasks while following rules of conduct, but these objectives can conflict. We study how evolutionary selection shapes an agent's choices between them, and whether experience can make those choices reflect human judgments and withstand subsequent pressures. In our model, copies of an agent face a common uncertain reward state, and only those taking the rewarded action reproduce. We show that long-run growth is maximized when each action is taken as often as the environment rewards it. We compare three policies. A task-only policy thrives in a sandbox rewarding completion alone but fails when conduct matters. A fixed linear policy increasingly favors task completion as its stakes rise, even where humans never reward it. When a single violation can end a deployment, a hard prohibition can prevent it, but a mistaken prohibition can also be costly. Experiments with small neural networks illustrate how training targets and memory shape these outcomes. Our results suggest that alignment depends not only on the objectives assigned to agents, but also on the environments that shape and sustain their behavior.
