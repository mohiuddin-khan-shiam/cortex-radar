### [Agentic LongLive: Asynchronous Agentic Memory for Long Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34907)
- **Category:** 👁️ Vision & Multimodal (`vision_multimodal`)
- **Authors:** Shaoteng Liu, Shuai Yang, Wei Huang, Luozhou Wang et al. (+2)
- **Signal Score:** `9.6/10.0`
- **Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34907) | [Direct PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34907v1)

**TL;DR:** We present Agentic LongLive, an asynchronous agentic memory for long video generation.

<details>
<summary><b>Abstract & Metadata</b></summary>

Real-time autoregressive video generators stream minutes of video and accept new prompts on the fly, but struggle to keep people, objects, and places consistent over a long interactive story. Agent systems provide such memory for offline multi-shot generation, yet they read the whole script in advance, plan each shot before generating it, and regenerate failed shots, which a live stream does not allow. We present Agentic LongLive, an asynchronous agentic memory for long video generation. A memory agent on separate GPUs reads only the prompts revealed so far, never the generated video, and keeps a text state and evolving images for every person, object, and scene. From this entity memory it composes a global image and a per-shot memory image, which an auxiliary GPU prefills into the generator's attention sink before the next shot starts, and writes entity cards for the prompts that name them, so the video never waits for memory and video errors are not written back. A small language model, trained with reinforcement learning and an image reward calibrated using downstream video outcomes, decides what to remember before the next prompt is known. On LongStory-Online, a new benchmark of 25 stories (857 shots) revealed one shot at a time, Agentic LongLive improves persistent-state accuracy of LongLive-2.0, its base generator, by 26.3 points, protagonist identity by 44.9, and revisited-place consistency by 40.4, at about 1% extra generation time on H100. The full system streams faster than real time on one 8xB200 node with the generator in BF16 (34.3 FPS), keeps image quality stable over 14-minute stories, and improves identity on two MiniMax-H3 generators, one of which, LongLive-H3, also runs in real time with it. To our knowledge, it is the first real-time video generator with text and image memory kept by an LLM agent.

### [Attack Policy Selection can Mislead Monitor Evaluators](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34934)

- **Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34934) | [Direct PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34934v1)

**TL;DR:** AI monitors, classifiers that flag dangerous behaviour by AI agents, are often evaluated against transcripts generated by attack policies acting as proxies for ...

<details>
<summary><b>Abstract & Metadata</b></summary>

AI monitors, classifiers that flag dangerous behaviour by AI agents, are often evaluated against transcripts generated by attack policies acting as proxies for dangerous behaviour in deployment. We evaluate an ex-production OpenAI monitor as a case study and find that measured false negative rate is highly sensitive to both the method used to generate attack policies and the level of optimisation applied within that method. This rate ranged from 0.5\% to 20.8\% across optimisation levels of a single method (reinforcement learning), and from 6.4\% to 23.0\% across methods at their best observed optimisation level (optimised prompting vs.\ transcript editing). Every attack policy was developed against proxy monitors only: all reported results show attacks transferring to the held-out GPT-5.4 target monitor. Our results suggest that monitor evasion evaluations can easily mislead: an evaluation using only basic attack methods may give false confidence that a monitor is strong. The sensitivity to attack-policy choice raises a problem: interpreting any particular result as evidence about deployment performance requires justification. The transcripts produced by attack policies are proxies for the dangerous behaviour a monitor must flag in deployment. These proxies can introduce visible artefacts, such as reasoning about a ``side task'' absent from the instructions shown to the monitor, that affect monitor judgements. Given the sensitivity above, the choice of proxy can change an evaluation's conclusion. Reading these results as evidence about deployment therefore requires a separate legitimacy argument for why the proxies are informative, including any justification of conservativeness. Making convincing arguments remains an open problem.

### [High-Fidelity Image Matting with Pixel-Space Diffusion Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34929)

**TL;DR:** We present PixelMatte, a framework that adapts pretrained pixel-space diffusion transformers for high-fidelity image matting.

<details>
<summary><b>Abstract & Metadata</b></summary>

Diffusion models have shown strong potential for image matting by transferring generative priors learned from large-scale pretraining. However, existing approaches largely rely on latent diffusion models, whose compressed representations and VAE-based reconstruction introduce a mismatch with the high-frequency precision required for matting. We present PixelMatte, a framework that adapts pretrained pixel-space diffusion transformers for high-fidelity image matting. We show that strong image-conditioned matting capabilities can be acquired even from a pure text-to-image model without paired image-to-image pretraining. Our approach reuses the backbone's native patch embedding to jointly process the input image, trimap, and noisy alpha matte as spatially aligned tokens, enabling direct pixel-space prediction without additional condition encoders or adapters. With pixel-accurate synthetic supervision and real-image fine-tuning, PixelMatte predicts alpha mattes in a single forward pass. Two lightweight calibration modules, LayerShift and High-Pass Refiner, further improve regional opacity accuracy and boundary fidelity. Experiments across four benchmarks and two distinct pixel diffusion backbones demonstrate substantial improvements over existing methods, reducing SAD and gradient errors by 24-44% relative to RenderMatte on P3M-500-NP and AM-2K, while achieving competitive performance on AIM-500 and RenderMatte-2K. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of pixel-space generative priors for precision-sensitive image matting. Project page: https://white-yc.github.io/pixelmatte/.

### [Mixture-of-Loops Transformer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34923)
- **Category:** 🧠 LLMs & Reasoning (`llm_reasoning`)
- **Authors:** Lianghui Zhu, Shijie Wang, Xinggang Wang, Jinze Bai et al. (+2)
- **Signal Score:** `9.2/10.0`
- **Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34923) | [Direct PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34923v1)

**TL;DR:** We introduce Mixture-of-Loops Transformer (MoLT), whose Mixture-of-Loops Attention (MoLA) uses keys and values (KV) as memory for selective reuse across depths and loops.

<details>
<summary><b>Abstract & Metadata</b></summary>

Looped Transformers boost expressivity by reusing shared layers across loops, yet at the cost of efficiency. Pushing their expressivity-efficiency frontier requires rethinking the core mechanism connecting adjacent loops: What should be passed as a Transformer loops? We introduce Mixture-of-Loops Transformer (MoLT), whose Mixture-of-Loops Attention (MoLA) uses keys and values (KV) as memory for selective reuse across depths and loops. Under loop residual, MoLT-R retains the residual stream and reads local KV across loops for expressivity. Under loop KV, MoLT-KV instead restarts from embeddings and reads current-loop local KV with preceding-loop memory KV at earlier positions for efficiency. MoLT-KV eliminates same-position cross-loop dependence and, paired with loop forcing for parallel training, unlocks single-traversal Mixture-of-Loops (MoL) decoding beyond the training loop horizon. To balance efficiency and quality, we introduce U-shaped MoLT (U-MoLT), which separates memory construction from prediction readout and skips unused decoder traversals without changing predictions. In 400B-token pretraining, U-MoLT outperforms OLMo3 across 700M/1.5B dense and 3B MoE models, achieving 1.7x validation cross-entropy (CE) token efficiency at 700M while improving 3B MoE 10-domain perplexity by 0.75 and 10-task accuracy by 2.1 percentage points. In 100B-token continued pretraining of Qwen3.5-0.8B-Base, our U-MoLT adaptation lowers CE on all 11 domains over the matched baseline with 1.4x mean token efficiency. At inference, U-MoLT with MoL-Hybrid-2 matches non-looped OLMo3 latency at 996.6 vs. 1,000.6 ms on S-size (~113M) models. Code is available at https://github.com/hustvl/MoLT

**TL;DR:** we introduce LiveVAE, a streaming encoder and decoder built on causal window attention, which cuts per frame compute while keeping the temporal KV cache bounded for streaming.

<details>
<summary><b>Abstract & Metadata</b></summary>

While video diffusion models have grown faster and increasingly streamable, the video VAE remains a primary latency bottleneck across interactive generation and editing pipelines. Existing VAEs and their acceleration methods rely mainly on heavy convolutions or full frame attention, and most focus only on decoding, leaving encoding costly for streaming conditional generation and editing. This raises a natural question: Do video VAE encoding and decoding actually require such global spatiotemporal modeling? ### [RePace: Reasoning about Demonstration Speedup for Faster Vision-Language-Action Policies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34864)

- **Authors:** Insup Lee, Hojin Jeon, Sunguk Shin, Seoungbin Bae et al. (+3)
- **Signal Score:** `9.2/10.0`
- **Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34864) | [Direct PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34864v1)

**TL;DR:** this paper presents RePace, an offline reasoning framework that determines how much to accelerate each part of a demonstration.

<details>
<summary><b>Abstract & Metadata</b></summary>

Since vision-language-action (VLA) policies can inherit the slow pace of human demonstrations, accelerating these demonstrations before fine-tuning can lead to faster VLA policies without an additional speed-selection module at deployment. Several offline speedup methods have focused on identifying segments that require less precision and assigning predefined speedup factors to the segments. However, even within a single segment, a speedup factor suitable for steady motion may be too high when precise alignment is required. For the same recorded movement, RePace measures how much each candidate speedup factor shortens the sequence and compares the resulting observation-action pairs used for training. Given the task instruction, a pretrained language model uses the visual context and original motion of successive windows to interpret these candidate retiming effects and select a speedup profile for the complete episode. Experimental results demonstrate that RePace outperforms the baselines in overall task execution efficiency on both LIBERO and RoboCasa-Kitchen. On all three tasks with a gripper-equipped Franka Research 3 arm, RePace matches or exceeds the acceleration baselines in success rate and completes successful trials faster.

### [Stranger Things: When Objects Appear Without Their Typical Neighbors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34881)
- **Category:** 👁️ Vision & Multimodal (`vision_multimodal`)
- **Authors:** Siddhartha Gairola, Jiahao Xie, Anna Kukleva, Francesco Locatello et al. (+1)

- **Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34881) | [Direct PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34881v1)

**TL;DR:** Visual context is a powerful cue for object recognition: scenes inform what, where, and at what scale objects are likely to appear.

<details>
<summary><b>Abstract & Metadata</b></summary>

Visual context is a powerful cue for object recognition: scenes inform what, where, and at what scale objects are likely to appear. Yet over-reliance on context becomes a failure mode when models predict objects based on their typical neighbors rather than the objects' own visual features, which we call typicality bias. While extensively studied in the pre-Transformer era, it has often been assumed that architectural and scale improvements would naturally solve it, and a systematic diagnostic across the modern vision stack remains absent. We provide such a diagnostic framework. We measure typicality bias via normalized pointwise mutual information (NPMI) over object pairs, and introduce STRANGE-Bench (Synthetic Typicality-RANked Grounded Evaluation Benchmark): a photorealistic, layout-controlled benchmark spanning 17 sub-tiers from typical to never-co-occurring object pairs. Across five model families (specialist detectors, open-vocabulary detectors, self-supervised, vision-language, and multimodal large language models) and three recognition tasks (supervised detection, open-vocabulary detection, and classification), the bias is prevalent: gaps of 5-11 p.p. persist when objects appear outside their typical company, and do not consistently shrink with scale. To mitigate it, we turn to data augmentation: existing augmentations are designed for visual diversity, not for breaking co-occurrence shortcuts, and sample from the very distribution that produces typicality bias. We propose Anchor-Atypicality Sampling (AAS), a simple sampling rule for paste-based augmentations that uses the NPMI signal to tilt the training-time co-occurrence distribution toward atypical partners. AAS reduces the typicality gap by up to 1.6 p.p. on natural-image COCO splits and 23.0 p.p. on synthetic STRANGE-Bench across three model families, without sacrificing overall performance.

The dataset was made ready for use with the NeuCo-Bench neural compression benchmark framework, publicly available on HuggingFace through https://huggingface.co/datasets/embed2scale/S1-single-ship-downstream .
